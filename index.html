Why we started Beacon

Some of the best things a student does happen where few people are watching. We wanted a way to recognize those things and tell students why they matter.

The good that can go unnoticed

At The Prayer Project, we started Beacon Scholars because we wanted to pay closer attention to the ways students already show up for people. Think of a student who notices a classmate eating alone and makes room at the table. Or one who helps get dinner on the table at home and still makes time for schoolwork. Those choices can shape someone’s day, and over time they can shape who a student becomes.

That’s what we mean by being a Beacon. It can be as simple as helping someone feel that they belong or being the person others know they can count on. Those things deserve to be named out loud, especially when the student doing them sees them as just part of the day.

Why saying it matters

It’s one thing to tell a student, “You’re doing a good job.” It’s another to say, “I remember what you did for that person, and here’s what it meant.” When someone takes the time to be that specific, a student gets to see something in themselves they might have missed. That can mean a lot in high school, when you’re still figuring out what you’re good at and how you want to use it.

We hope students come away from Beacon with a clearer sense of what they bring to the people around them. We hope the adults and friends in their lives come away paying closer attention, too. Some students have been quietly doing good for a long time before anyone thinks to ask them about it.

What we look for in an entry

Reviewers read your entry for examples of the following qualities. You might recognize several of them in one story.

Character and integrity
You keep your word, own up to mistakes, and treat people with care even when it would be easier to look away.
Perseverance and growth
You can explain what changed in you after a setback, a difficult year, or a responsibility you had to grow into.
Service and compassion
You notice what other people need and make an effort to help. That might happen at home, at school, at work, or through organized service.
Leadership and initiative
You see something that needs doing and take responsibility for it. The story can be as simple as including someone or helping a group follow through.
Purpose and potential
You can tell us what you hope to do next, what draws you to it, and how you hope it will benefit the people around you.

How selection works

A nomination and a direct application are both official entries and receive the same consideration.

  1. Submit an application or nomination

    Students can apply themselves or have someone submit a nomination on their behalf. If you think you were nominated, check by name and request a connection to your Beacon account. You can check the submission and follow-up steps in My Dashboard.

  2. Processing

    The committee confirms each entry is connected to the right student account. If you were nominated, sign in or create your own account after checking by name. Once the connection is confirmed, complete the follow-up in My Dashboard.

  3. Verify eligibility

    Beacon checks that the student is in grades 9–12 and has an unweighted GPA of at least 2.5. The team can follow up for details missing from a nomination. Have an academic and a character reference ready, and ask a parent or guardian to complete the consent form if you’re under 18.

  4. Committee review

    Reviewers read the application or nomination and consider the student’s character, perseverance, compassion, initiative, and plans for the future. A strong entry helps reviewers understand what the student did, what they learned, and why it mattered to the people involved.

  5. Finalist round

    Students who advance to the final round earn the distinction of Beacon Finalist. Eligibility, both references, and guardian consent when required must be verified before a student advances. Finalists with linked entries can see their status in My Dashboard.

  6. Beacon Scholars

    Two finalists will be named Beacon Scholars for the Class of 2027. Each will receive an official letter and certificate from The Prayer Project and access to a private Scholar Passport. Students can see the final decision in their dashboard.

What to do while you wait

Check your dashboard and the inbox tied to your Beacon account. If a reference or consent form is still pending, check that the person you listed received their invitation. The application and nomination deadline applies to new submissions. You can still complete follow-up on an entry already submitted and under consideration.

Common questions

Who can apply, and how do I get started?

Students in grades 9–12 with an unweighted GPA of at least 2.5 can apply. Create an account, verify your email, and fill out the application. You can save a draft and come back before the deadline.

Start an application →
What should I write about in my essay?

Pick an experience you remember clearly. Tell us what was happening, what you did, and what you learned. You can write about caring for someone, taking responsibility for a mistake, keeping up with school through a change, or showing initiative in a place you know well.

What do I do if I received a nomination email or letter?

Check for your nomination by name. After the committee connects it to your Beacon account, open it in My Dashboard and add any outstanding references. If you’re under 18, enter your parent or guardian’s information so they can complete a separate consent form.

Continue your nomination →
Can I nominate someone privately?

Yes. A named nomination allows Beacon to share your name with the student. With a confidential nomination, Beacon administrators keep your name private. You can also submit anonymously without signing in. Provide a delivery route that will reach the student or their parent or guardian.

Who should I ask to be a reference?

Ask one person who knows your schoolwork and one who can speak to your character. A qualified nominator can sometimes serve as one reference after Beacon verifies their identity and accepts their statement. Anonymous nominations still need two separate references.

Do I need volunteer hours to apply?

No. Community service is optional. You can tell us about the responsibilities you carry, the way you care for people, or something difficult that helped you grow.

How many students will be named Beacon Scholars?

Two students will be named Beacon Scholars for the inaugural Class of 2027. Finalist is the distinction for reaching the last round of consideration.

Read about both honors →
When does a parent or guardian need to sign?

If you are under 18, a parent or legal guardian needs to complete a separate authorization form. Enter their contact information in your application or nomination follow-up. Beacon verifies the response before you can advance to Finalist.

Can I finish follow-up after the deadline?

Yes. The intake deadline applies to new applications and nominations. If your entry was submitted on time and is still under consideration, you can connect a nomination or complete requested follow-up. References and guardians should use the private links sent to them before those links expire.

Where will I see whether I became a finalist?

Open your submitted entry in My Dashboard. If your entry advances, you’ll see your Finalist status there. If you’re named a Beacon Scholar, your dashboard also links to your Scholar Passport.

PROGRAM PARTICIPATION

Privacy, care & integrity.

This is an honorary recognition program of The Prayer Project. No financial assistance, award amount, future scholarship, or academic accreditation is promised.

Nominations are valid official entries; students do not need to reapply. Final selection requires eligibility verification, two verified references and parent/guardian consent for minors. Committee reviews are confidential; donors do not select recipients.

Nomination privacy choices include named, confidential, and fully anonymous. Public recognition and photos are optional and separately authorized. Private addresses, references and guardian records are never included in public profiles.

Eligibility and consent

Beacon considers students in grades 9–12 with an unweighted GPA of at least 2.5. Service is optional. If a student is under 18, Beacon verifies a parent or guardian’s authorization before final recognition.

How Beacon uses your information

Beacon uses entries to verify eligibility, follow up with students and authorized adults, review entries, and deliver recognition. Share only information relevant to the entry. Avoid including private health information, home addresses, or school records in an essay or nomination.

Corrections and withdrawal

Students can check their entry in the Beacon dashboard. Contact The Prayer Project to request a correction, ask about your records, or withdraw guardian consent. Include your entry ID when available and keep private documents out of your initial message.
